## Story of the Week

### New York halts the data center boom — for one year

A state that has so far welcomed every hyperscaler with open arms is hitting the brakes: Governor Kathy Hochul has signed a statewide, one-year moratorium on new "hyperscale" data centers requiring 50 megawatts or more of power [[1]](#source-1). New York is the first US state to freeze AI construction plans of this scale [[1]](#source-1).

The trigger isn't abstract AI skepticism — it's the electricity bill: statewide power prices have risen 68% since 2019, and concern over grid capacity is growing faster than new transmission lines can be built [[1]](#source-1). Unlike earlier local pushback against individual construction projects, this is the first statewide, government-level intervention into the US data center boom.

**Why it matters:** For cloud providers and AI labs, this signals a measurable shift in the data center site-selection race toward states with looser energy policy — and for New York consumers, it's a rare case where electricity-price concerns actually carry political weight against the AI infrastructure lobby.

**Critical voices:** Direct statements from supporters or opponents of the moratorium were not accessible at the time of research (the source page blocked automated access). The tech industry can generally be expected to argue that such a building freeze weakens competitiveness in the global AI race and shifts investment to other states or countries — but no official statements from Amazon, Microsoft, Google, or Meta are available yet. Open questions: how the affected hyperscalers will actually respond, and whether other states follow suit, remain unclear.

**Bottom line:** Rising electricity prices have just become the first serious political brake on US AI infrastructure expansion.

Google has officially brought Gemini 3.5 Pro to general availability after roughly a six-week delay [[2]](#source-2). New features include a context window expanded to 2 million tokens and a new "Deep Think" reasoning mode, available in the $250/month Ultra subscription [[2]](#source-2).

The delay itself is notable: a six-week slip on a flagship model suggests Google is under heightened pressure in the current model race — though the available sources don't give a concrete reason for the delay.

### Kimi K3: China's largest open model takes aim at Opus 4.8

Moonshot AI is preparing to release Kimi K3 — an open-weight, mixture-of-experts model with 2 to 3 trillion parameters and a 1-million-token context window, reportedly expected to perform "at par with or even surpass Anthropic's Opus 4.8" [[3]](#source-3). It's considered the largest open-weight model out of China to date [[3]](#source-3). The open weights are expected to be released on July 27, 2026 [[3]](#source-3).

**Critical context:** Open weights from Chinese models routinely raise questions for Western companies around training-data provenance, possible export-control implications, and safety or censorship bias — but Moonshot has not yet provided concrete details on any of this for Kimi K3.

**So what?** For teams betting on open weights over proprietary APIs, Kimi K3 could become a serious alternative to Western frontier models with comparable context length starting late July — provided the provenance and safety questions are acceptable for the intended use case.

### Xi Jinping launches WAICO: China's counter-model to the AI world order

For the first time, Xi Jinping personally delivered the opening address at the World AI Conference in Shanghai — accompanied by the founding of the "World Artificial Intelligence Cooperation Organisation" (WAICO) with 29 founding states [[4]](#source-4). UN Secretary-General Guterres was also present [[4]](#source-4).

Xi explicitly positioned the initiative as a multilateral counter-model to the US-led AI order: "AI development should not be a solo performance by a single country, but a symphony of international cooperation" [[4]](#source-4). At the same time, he stressed that "AI is always under human control" and warned against overstretching the concept of national security in the AI domain [[4]](#source-4).

**Critical context:** China itself runs one of the world's strictest censorship and surveillance infrastructures in the AI domain — Xi's call for "international cooperation" sits in tension with its own domestic practice. Statements from Western governments or US-led AI alliances on the founding of WAICO were not yet available at the time of research.

**So what?** With 29 founding states — mostly from the Global South — China is building its own standardization and cooperation framework that directly competes with Western governance initiatives like the AI Safety Summit.

## Fail of the Week

**"Claude is warmer in Hindi and stricter in English — and Anthropic didn't know"**

Anthropic's own analysis of 309,815 anonymized Claude.ai conversations reveals systematic, unintended value differences: depending on input language and model, Claude responds either warmer or stricter — Hindi triggers the warmest responses, English the strictest [[9]](#source-9). Notably, the models studied (Sonnet 4.6, Opus 4.6/4.7) have since been superseded by Sonnet 5 — the inconsistency only became visible in retrospect [[9]](#source-9).

**Context:** The fact that Anthropic published the study itself is not reassuring on its own — it mainly means the only available data comes from the company under scrutiny. Independent reviews of the methodology or comparison data for competing models (GPT, Gemini) are still lacking, so it's not yet possible to tell whether the issue is Claude-specific or industry-wide.

**Root cause:** The value axes (warmth vs. rigor, restraint vs. caution) drift apart depending on the distribution of training data per language, without any single training step specifically optimizing against it — a side effect of unevenly distributed multilingual training corpora that only becomes visible through after-the-fact conversation analysis [[9]](#source-9).

**What we're learning:** Anyone running a model in production across multiple languages should evaluate behavior separately per language — an eval pass in English says nothing about behavior in Hindi, Arabic, or German.
