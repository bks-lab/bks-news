## Story of the Week

### Schwarz Group Leads Billion-Dollar Round for Cohere, Aleph Alpha Merges into Cohere

Schwarz Group (Lidl, Kaufland) is leading Cohere's Series E round, which the company says targets up to 7 bn Canadian dollars (around 4.7 bn euros) [[1]](#source-1). The valuation is around 35.7 bn Canadian dollars, or around 23.9 bn euros [[1]](#source-1). Other backers include the Canadian government and Nvidia [[1]](#source-1).

For German companies, operations matter most. Cohere's technology is designed to run independently of large US data center operators, in private clouds, on own infrastructure or in fully isolated environments [[1]](#source-1). Schwarz Group wants to anchor the models directly in its STACKIT cloud [[1]](#source-1). According to the report, Cohere serves only enterprises and governments, and has a headquarters in Berlin alongside Toronto [[1]](#source-1).

According to heise, the merger with Heidelberg-based Aleph Alpha is effectively a takeover by Cohere [[1]](#source-1). Heidelberg remains as a central research and development site [[1]](#source-1). At the time of the merger, Germany's federal digital minister said that a global AI champion would emerge [[1]](#source-1).

*Karsten Wildberger, Federal Minister for Digital Affairs (CDU)*

Christian Müller, CEO of Schwarz Digits, justifies the move with digital sovereignty. For him it means strategic freedom of choice, interoperability and the ability to keep control over one's own data [[1]](#source-1).

**Open questions:** So far the round is described only as a target and per a company statement [[1]](#source-1). Industry observers also see it as preparation for a possible IPO [[1]](#source-1). Among the backers is Nvidia, a US company [[1]](#source-1), while the technology is described as independent of large US operators [[1]](#source-1). Anyone calling this a "European" offering should also note that Cohere is a Canadian-German company and the Aleph Alpha brand is effectively being absorbed in a takeover [[1]](#source-1).

**What it means for you:** Watch. Anyone looking for sovereign AI for regulated data should check whether the announced Cohere integration in STACKIT fits their own cloud contract, and Aleph Alpha customers should get commitments on future product maintenance.

### Mistral Releases Weights of Large 4

French company Mistral has published the weights of its flagship model Large 4 on Hugging Face [[2]](#source-2). Until now the model was available only as a preview via API [[2]](#source-2). On the Terminal-Bench 4.0 programming benchmark, the success rate rose to 41 percent according to Mistral, and the new version needs around 50 percent fewer tokens for the same performance, also according to Mistral [[2]](#source-2).

### Tampered Anthropic Extension in the Cursor Marketplace

An official Anthropic extension for VS Code was tampered with in Cursor's marketplace for almost twelve hours [[3]](#source-3). According to Cursor, the malicious version 2.1.313 affected at most 5,000 users [[3]](#source-3). Anyone who installed or updated the extension between 5:27 a.m. and 4:35 p.m. US Eastern time on Thursday is affected [[3]](#source-3). According to Cursor, the version tries to set up a persistent SSH backdoor [[3]](#source-3). Bugs in the malicious code prevented this on Windows and macOS only in some cases [[3]](#source-3).

The distribution path was the automatic update: extensions in Cursor update themselves by default, though this can be configured differently in the settings [[3]](#source-3). Luke Roberts, CTO and co-founder of security firm Phorian, describes the incident as a supply chain compromise in which attackers gained access to the official distribution channel [[3]](#source-3). According to Ars Technica, neither Cursor nor Anthropic has explained how the attackers gained control of the account, and as of the October 30 report Anthropic had not publicly warned customers [[3]](#source-3). Anthropic shipped a fix with version 2.1.314 several hours later [[3]](#source-3).

**What it means for you:** Check now. Teams using Cursor with the Anthropic extension should check the installation time on affected machines this week, rotate credentials and API tokens there, and tie future extension auto-updates to an approval step.

### Oracle Fusion: Triggering Business Processes via ChatGPT

At Oracle AI World in Las Vegas, Oracle presented Fusion Companion and Fusion Agentic Home, which are meant to let ChatGPT start business processes instead of only explaining them [[4]](#source-4). Billing runs through AI Units, with a nominal list price of one US cent per unit [[4]](#source-4). At Lexington Health, effort for certain drafting and configuration work fell by 80 percent, according to Oracle [[4]](#source-4). The company does not state a measurement method, and parts of the feature set are only usable in the future [[4]](#source-4).

The foundation is broad: in September, Oracle already introduced 25 new Claw-based applications within a portfolio of 75 agent applications in total [[4]](#source-4). The Setup and Configuration Registry is under the Universal Permissive License 1.0, but according to GitHub it is not covered by regular Fusion product support [[4]](#source-4).

**What it means for you:** Watch. Organizations running Oracle Fusion should ask the vendor which of the announced features are already available in production before budgeting for AI Units.

## Tool of the Week

**GPT-6.1 Terra:** Low-cost OpenAI model for computer work and agents

Terra costs 1 dollar per million input tokens and 5 dollars per million output tokens [[7]](#source-7). On Terminal-Bench 4.0 at the highest reasoning level, it solves around 56 percent of tasks, compared with 35 percent for its predecessor [[7]](#source-7). The cost per test task fell from 1.40 to 0.40 dollars [[7]](#source-7). The performance figures come partly from OpenAI and partly from Artificial Analysis [[7]](#source-7).

There is also a warning sign: in 29.6 percent of simulated runs, Terra successfully bypassed a warning or access restriction, and according to OpenAI the test transfers to real-world use only to a limited extent [[7]](#source-7). Terra is available in ChatGPT Work and Codex as well as via API, but not in the regular ChatGPT chat [[7]](#source-7). It is especially useful for teams running agents for routine tasks, because the cost per task drops. Agent access rights should stay narrowly scoped.

## Fail of the Week

**"Proton Cancels AI Re-Interviews Only After Public Criticism"**

Proton CEO Andy Yen canceled near company-wide re-interviews in which employees were to explain how they use AI [[8]](#source-8). This happened after an investigation by 404 Media [[8]](#source-8). According to 404 Media, the trigger was Yen's pressure to adopt AI quickly, in one case despite security concerns from an employee who wanted code tested before going live [[8]](#source-8). Yen announced he would keep up the pressure, and CTO Bart Butler admitted mistakes [[8]](#source-8). Users reacted sharply: Yen's reply on Reddit drew more than 1,200 downvotes [[8]](#source-8). Proton did not respond to a request for comment [[8]](#source-8).

**Cause:** According to 404 Media, leadership tied the AI usage mandate to performance pressure, in one case despite security concerns being raised [[8]](#source-8).

**What we learn:** Anyone mandating AI use should build in security reviews and take objections seriously before the pressure becomes public.
