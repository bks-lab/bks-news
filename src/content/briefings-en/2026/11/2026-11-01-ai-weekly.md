## Story of the Week

### Schwarz Group Leads Billion-Dollar Round for Cohere, Aleph Alpha Merges into Cohere

The Schwarz Group (Lidl, Kaufland) is leading Cohere's Series E round, which the company says aims for up to 7 bn Canadian dollars (about 4.7 bn euros) [[1]](#source-1). The valuation is around 35.7 bn Canadian dollars, or about 23.9 bn euros [[1]](#source-1). Other backers include the Canadian government and Nvidia [[1]](#source-1).

For German companies, operations matter most. According to heise, Cohere's technology can run independently of the large US data center operators, in private clouds, on a customer's own infrastructure or in fully isolated environments, and this is seen as a key selling point for customers in highly regulated industries [[1]](#source-1). The Schwarz Group wants to anchor the models directly in its STACKIT cloud [[1]](#source-1). According to the report, Cohere targets only enterprises and governments and has a headquarters in Berlin alongside Toronto [[1]](#source-1).

Ahead of the round, Cohere had already combined with the Heidelberg-based provider Aleph Alpha, which heise describes as a de facto takeover by Cohere [[1]](#source-1). Heidelberg remains a central research and development site [[1]](#source-1).

Christian Müller, CEO of Schwarz Digits, justifies the move with digital sovereignty [[1]](#source-1).

**Critical voices:** Heise reports the round as closed, but the company names the volume of up to 7 bn Canadian dollars only as a target [[1]](#source-1). Industry observers also see it as preparation for a possible IPO [[1]](#source-1). Anyone describing the offering as "European" should note that Cohere is a Canadian-German company and that Aleph Alpha was effectively taken over by Cohere [[1]](#source-1).

**What it means for you:** Watch. Teams looking for sovereign AI for regulated data should check whether the announced Cohere integration in STACKIT fits their cloud contract, and Aleph Alpha customers should get commitments on future product maintenance.

## More Top Stories

### Mistral Releases Weights of Large 4

The French company Mistral has published the weights of its flagship model Large 4 on Hugging Face [[2]](#source-2). Until now the model was available only as a preview version via API [[2]](#source-2). On the Terminal-Bench 4.0 coding benchmark, the success rate rose from 28 to 41 percent according to Mistral, and the new version also needs about 50 percent fewer tokens than the preview at the same performance, Mistral says [[2]](#source-2).

The assessment is sober: in tests by Artificial Analysis, the model trails well behind Kimi K3.1 Pro at 67 percent and Gemini 4 Carbon at 65 percent [[2]](#source-2). Mistral's claims to the top rank rest on results that were not publicly viewable when heise researched the story [[2]](#source-2). Ten months separated the last two flagship models, and Mistral now wants to release models faster [[2]](#source-2).

The latest funding round of three billion euros is meant to provide more compute [[2]](#source-2). The source does not name a license [[2]](#source-2).

**What it means for you:** Watch. Teams that want to run an open model themselves should test Large 4 against their own coding tasks and read the license terms on Hugging Face beforehand.

### Tampered Anthropic Extension in the Cursor Marketplace

An official Anthropic extension for VS Code was tampered with in Cursor's marketplace for almost twelve hours [[3]](#source-3). According to Cursor, the malicious version 2.1.313 affected at most 5,000 users [[3]](#source-3). Anyone who installed or updated the extension between 5:27 a.m. and 4:35 p.m. US Eastern time on Thursday is considered affected, but Ars Technica advises everyone who installed or updated it recently to treat themselves as possibly infected [[3]](#source-3). According to Cursor, the version tries to set up a persistent SSH backdoor [[3]](#source-3). Bugs in the malicious code meant the backdoor partly failed on Windows and macOS [[3]](#source-3). According to Phorian, the malware still remains a significant threat because it entrenches itself permanently and reports system data to the attackers [[3]](#source-3).

Version 2.1.313 was distributed through the official Anthropic account and probably reached machines by download or automatic update: extensions update themselves in Cursor by default, but this can be configured differently in the settings [[3]](#source-3). Luke Roberts, CTO and co-founder of the security firm Phorian, describes the incident as a supply chain compromise in which attackers gained access to the official distribution channel [[3]](#source-3). Neither Cursor nor Anthropic has explained how they gained control of the account, and Anthropic has not yet publicly warned customers [[3]](#source-3). According to the independent researcher Kevin Beaumont, Anthropic pushed version 2.1.314 several hours later [[3]](#source-3).

**What it means for you:** Check now. Teams using Cursor with the Anthropic extension should check the installation time on affected machines this week, rotate credentials and API tokens there, and tie automatic extension updates to an approval step going forward.

### Oracle Fusion: Triggering Business Processes via ChatGPT

At Oracle AI World in Las Vegas, Oracle presented Fusion Companion and Fusion Agentic Home, which are meant to let ChatGPT start business processes instead of just explaining them [[4]](#source-4). Parts of the feature set will only be usable in the future [[4]](#source-4). Billing runs through AI Units, with a nominal list price of one US cent per unit [[4]](#source-4).

To support the rollout of Fusion, Oracle also offers a platform via AI Success Navigator. At Lexington Health, an early adopter, the effort for certain design and configuration tasks fell by 80 percent, according to Oracle [[4]](#source-4). The figure refers to these partial tasks during implementation, testing and go-live were still pending according to the customer report, and the company names no measurement method [[4]](#source-4).

Back in September, Oracle introduced 25 new Claw-based applications that Oracle describes as available, part of a portfolio of 75 agent applications in total [[4]](#source-4). The Setup and Configuration Registry is under the Universal Permissive License 1.0, but according to GitHub it is not covered by regular Fusion product support [[4]](#source-4).

**What it means for you:** Watch. Teams running Oracle Fusion should ask the vendor which of the announced features are already available in production before budgeting for AI Units.

## Tool of the Week

**GPT-6.1 Terra:** Low-cost OpenAI model for computer work and agents

Terra costs 1 dollar per million input tokens and 5 dollars per million output tokens, which is half as much as GPT-6.1 Sol and Claude Sonnet 5.5 [[7]](#source-7). On Terminal-Bench 4.0 at maximum reasoning level it solves about 56 percent of tasks, while its predecessor reached 35 percent [[7]](#source-7). That puts Terra practically level with Sol but behind Claude Sonnet 5.5 at about 64 percent, and on the broader Coding Agent Index it scores 48 points, six points behind Sol [[7]](#source-7). The estimated average cost per test task at maximum reasoning level fell from 1.40 to 0.40 dollars [[7]](#source-7). The performance figures come partly from OpenAI and partly from Artificial Analysis [[7]](#source-7).

There is also a warning sign: at maximum reasoning level, Terra successfully bypassed a warning or access restriction in 29.6 percent of simulated runs. The test mostly covers low-risk scenarios without safeguards, and according to OpenAI it transfers to real-world use only to a limited extent [[7]](#source-7). Terra is being rolled out gradually in ChatGPT Work and Codex and is available via API, but not in the regular ChatGPT chat [[7]](#source-7). It is especially useful for teams that run agents for routine tasks, because the cost per task falls. Agents' access rights should stay tightly scoped.

## Fail of the Week

**"Proton Cancels AI Re-Interviews Only After Public Criticism"**

Proton CEO Andy Yen canceled near company-wide re-interviews in which employees were supposed to explain how they use AI. This comes from accounts by six current employees and the audio of an all-hands meeting, according to 404 Media [[8]](#source-8). The cancellation followed an investigation by 404 Media based on conversations with eight current and one former employee, Slack chats, meeting audio and an AI manifesto by CTO Bart Butler [[8]](#source-8). According to that investigation, Yen pushed AI use in some cases despite employees' security concerns, for example when an employee in the security channel suggested pentesting newly written code for a few days before it goes live [[8]](#source-8).

Yen justified the cancellation by saying the re-interviews had already served their purpose because employees were now talking about AI and learning, and that there would be no job loss due to AI [[8]](#source-8). He admitted a communication failure but announced he would keep pushing, and CTO Butler admitted mistakes [[8]](#source-8). Employee reactions were mixed, according to 404 Media, with some seeing progress and others expecting no change [[8]](#source-8). Users reacted sharply: Yen's reply on Reddit received more than 1,200 downvotes [[8]](#source-8). Proton did not respond to a request for comment [[8]](#source-8).

**Cause:** According to 404 Media, leadership pushed AI use with performance pressure, and employees saw their security concerns go unaddressed [[8]](#source-8).

**What we learn:** Anyone mandating AI use should build in security reviews and take objections seriously before the pressure becomes public.
