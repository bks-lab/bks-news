## Story of the Week

### OpenAI acquires Pierre Computer and builds a rival to GitHub

OpenAI has announced the acquisition of Pierre Computer [[1]](#source-1). According to heise, OpenAI has already built parts of Pierre's tools into Codex, including the Git infrastructure Code Storage [[1]](#source-1). With the acquisition, OpenAI may be entering another area of friction with GitHub, according to heise, and Codex is slowly approaching its functions [[1]](#source-1). For context: Microsoft and OpenAI also compete in other fields, which heise describes as not unusual in the AI industry [[1]](#source-1).

OpenAI gave no information on the price [[1]](#source-1). Pierre's open source projects remain openly accessible after closing [[1]](#source-1). On the rationale, heise reports OpenAI's position as follows: the systems around Git have to keep evolving [[1]](#source-1).

The move hits a market that Microsoft occupies: in 2018 Microsoft bought GitHub for 7.5 billion US dollars [[1]](#source-1), and Microsoft currently holds about a quarter of the shares in OpenAI [[1]](#source-1). This week OpenAI also presented "Pando", a backend for Git repositories [[1]](#source-1). In addition, GitHub's former CEO has launched a GitHub alternative called Entire [[1]](#source-1).

**Critical voices:** According to heise, critics say GitHub has become less reliable lately and point to an outage that disrupted Actions, Pages and billing [[1]](#source-1). Heise cites them via Axios. What the acquisition costs remains open [[1]](#source-1).

**What it means for you:** Watch. Engineering leads who keep their source code on GitHub should clarify by year end which dependencies on repository, CI and coding agents they might move later.

## More Top Stories

### German government AI task force sets targets for 2030

The German government is setting clear targets for 2030: at least 80 percent of large companies and 55 percent of small and medium-sized businesses are to use AI by then [[2]](#source-2). The task force report ends with a list of 18 key measures [[2]](#source-2). These are government targets, not measured values.

Public administration is also meant to deliver: by 2030, 90 percent of federal agencies are to use at least one AI application for core tasks [[2]](#source-2). The report warns that the window to narrow the gap is closing soon [[2]](#source-2).

Heise, however, considers the report to be hardly critical of AI [[2]](#source-2).

**What it means for you:** Watch. Managing directors at mid-sized companies should treat the 55 percent target as a political benchmark that depends on broad participation, and check where their own company stands today as part of their AI planning.

### Protests against AI data centers reach Europe

In Dublin around 2,000 people marched according to the broadcaster RTE [[3]](#source-3), and in Helsinki about 1,300 gathered according to YLE [[3]](#source-3). On November 7, the group Stop Dirty Data Centres protested at a Microsoft data center near Køge in Denmark [[3]](#source-3).

No location in Germany, Austria or Switzerland appears in the report [[3]](#source-3). Still, for operators and users it shows that the siting and energy demand of data centers are being negotiated in public across Europe.

**What it means for you:** Watch. IT leads who buy data center capacity or AI services from Europe should add location and energy source to their provider selection.

## Fail of the Week

**Meta pauses terms change after criticism**

Meta wanted to introduce new commercial terms, and after criticism the company suspended the change on October 28 [[7]](#source-7). According to Ars Technica, the change affects operators of business Facebook pages, who would have had to allow Meta to crawl linked external sites [[7]](#source-7). The changes were originally due to apply on October 30, and Meta had already pushed the date to November 20 [[7]](#source-7). According to Nieman Lab, courts in Brazil and Norway issued preliminary injunctions against the terms [[7]](#source-7). In addition, the UK competition authority CMA opened a formal proceeding [[7]](#source-7).

**Cause:** The terms were under pressure from courts, a regulator and the public before they took effect [[7]](#source-7). Meta denies circumventing robots.txt and says its crawling practice remains unchanged [[7]](#source-7).

**What we learn:** Anyone changing terms of use for AI data should involve those affected and the regulator beforehand, or courts will stop it later.
