## More Top Stories

### Windows 11 migration stalls over "chipflation" and "RAMageddon"

In September, Windows 10 still ran on 27.7 percent of Windows PCs worldwide [[3]](#source-3). According to CFO Amy Hood, Microsoft's Windows revenue from PC makers fell 27 percent in the latest quarter [[3]](#source-3). PC shipments dropped 21 percent in the third quarter, driven by high memory prices, uncertainty over tariffs and the Iran war [[3]](#source-3). For the fourth quarter of 2026, one analyst expects a 27 percent decline year on year [[3]](#source-3).

Dean McCarron, principal analyst at Mercury Research, describes the mechanism as follows: when prices rise, demand falls, and the replacement cycle shifts backward [[3]](#source-3).

According to Hyperframe Research, memory supply shortages could last until 2029 [[3]](#source-3). The 27.7 percent share comes from StatCounter measurement data, and only the fourth-quarter forecast and the shortage expectation are analyst estimates [[3]](#source-3).

**What it means for you:** Check now. IT leads should secure hardware orders for the Windows 11 migration with price quotes now and list the Windows 10 devices that are still running.

### Smart Reports: Anthropic gives admins insight into Claude usage

With "Smart Reports", authorized managers can have Claude usage in the company analyzed [[4]](#source-4). According to the company, the feature is free for Enterprise customers but disabled by default, and each company can create up to ten reports per month [[4]](#source-4). In a help page article, Anthropic describes the analysis this way: Claude reads a sample of the transcripts in that scope, groups them by workflow and output type, assigns the matching costs to each group and summarizes the results [[4]](#source-4).

The background is cost concern: according to a Bitkom survey, a third of companies in Germany said they were surprised by the high costs of their AI use [[4]](#source-4).

**What it means for you:** Watch. Anyone using Claude Enterprise should clarify with data protection and the works council which analyses are permitted before activating it.

### Study: Water demand of German data centers could more than double by 2030

German data centers needed around 13 billion liters of water for cooling in 2025, and by 2030 demand could grow to up to 27 billion liters per year [[5]](#source-5). This is the result of a short study by the Öko-Institut commissioned by Greenpeace [[5]](#source-5). The weighted average is 0.67 liters of water per kilowatt hour of IT power consumption [[5]](#source-5).

The study warns that new mega data centers could cause acute groundwater stress and distribution conflicts in regions that are already dry [[5]](#source-5). It contrasts sharply with the digital industry's account: the industry association Bitkom had shortly before rated water demand as largely unproblematic [[5]](#source-5).

**What it means for you:** Watch. Anyone tendering colocation or cloud contracts or compiling sustainability reports should ask providers for water consumption per site.

## Fail of the Week

**"Meta pauses controversial commercial terms"**

Meta has paused an update to its commercial terms of use indefinitely [[9]](#source-9). It was originally due to take effect on October 30, and the date was moved to November 20 [[9]](#source-9). Courts in Brazil and Norway issued preliminary injunctions, and the UK competition authority CMA opened a formal investigation [[9]](#source-9). A Meta spokesperson told Reuters that the existing terms remain in force [[9]](#source-9). Meta stresses that it never intended to use the terms of use to get around robots.txt [[9]](#source-9).

**Cause:** The change met judicial and regulatory resistance before it could take effect [[9]](#source-9).

**What we learn:** Anyone changing vendor terms on data use and AI training must expect pushback, and customers should actively read change notices.

## Number of the Week

The Vals platform measured the token cost of an attack in simulated networks: 2 to 3 dollars with DeepSeek, 17 to 46 dollars with GPT-6-Sol [[10]](#source-10). The values concern different models, and according to the source, open models are generally cheaper because they run on your own hardware [[10]](#source-10). DeepSeek met 73 percent of the objectives on average, and OpenAI's Daybreak completed 81 percent of the tasks [[10]](#source-10). Nikil Ravi, a researcher at Vals, says the best models, which sit in the labs and are available only through access programs, are now being caught up with by open models that anyone can download [[10]](#source-10). These are measurements in simulations, not real attacks, and Wired points out that real attackers mostly use older models [[10]](#source-10).
