## Story of the Week

### EU Countries Open Up AI Training to Personal Data

The EU Council wants to allow AI training with personal data even without pseudonymization when it serves the companies' legitimate interest [[1]](#source-1). EU countries agreed on this on October 21 in their position on the Data Omnibus, as the Irish Council presidency announced [[1]](#source-1). The legal basis is to remain Article 6 of the GDPR, including a right to object [[1]](#source-1).

The Council also wants to drop the right of access for unfounded or excessive requests [[1]](#source-1). On cookies, it rejects the Commission's proposal of a central choice, for example in the browser, and instead extends the list of purposes that need no consent [[1]](#source-1). Under the Data Act, the protection of trade secrets is to be strengthened [[1]](#source-1). Parliament is to find its position in February, after which trilogue negotiations begin [[1]](#source-1).

Germany voted against the Council position together with Austria and Estonia [[1]](#source-1). On the cookie plan not to ask users again for four months after a decision, Germany considered three months sufficient [[1]](#source-1). According to the Federal Ministry for Digital Affairs, the text falls short [[1]](#source-1). A spokesperson said that better protection of trade secrets and simplifications for SMEs under the GDPR were red lines for Germany [[1]](#source-1).

*Spokesperson, Federal Ministry for Digital Affairs*

**Critical voices:** According to netzpolitik.org, the NGO noyb speaks of digital expropriation [[1]](#source-1). The netzpolitik.org author calls the legal basis of legitimate interest shaky [[1]](#source-1). This account rests on netzpolitik.org's reporting.

**What it means for you:** Watch. Anyone who trains or commissions AI models using customer or employee data should wait for Parliament's position in February and not base any processes on the Council text until then.

### Meta Terms: Publishers Demand EU Stop Over AI Scraping

According to heise, Meta plans to update its commercial terms for business accounts on October 30 [[4]](#source-4). The associations EPC, BDZV and MVFP are asking the EU Commission to review and prohibit the change, and see a violation of the Digital Markets Act [[4]](#source-4). Meta removed the explicit wording on training AI models while other automated uses remain, which publishers see as a diversion [[4]](#source-4). Meta disputes this [[4]](#source-4). The British CMA plans to summon Meta over the changes [[4]](#source-4).

**What it means for you:** Check now. Anyone running Facebook or Instagram business accounts and publishing their own content there should read the new terms before October 30 and get sign-off from the legal department.

### PHP: BSI Warns of Critical Vulnerabilities

All known PHP versions, going back to at least PHP 5, are vulnerable according to the BSI [[2]](#source-2). The developers released patches on October 21 at 4 p.m. German time [[2]](#source-2). The BSI cannot rule out remote code execution [[2]](#source-2). Vice President Thomas Caspers expects rapid exploitation because attackers can analyze the updates with AI tools. He said the BSI considers short-term, large-scale exploitation within a few hours of the security updates' release likely [[2]](#source-2).

*Thomas Caspers, BSI Vice President*

**What it means for you:** Check now. Anyone running PHP web servers should apply the available patches immediately and review the logs of recent days for anomalies.

## Tool of the Week

**Migration kit for single-agent architectures:** Anthropic's help for merging many subagents into one main agent with skills.

Anthropic provides the kit on GitHub, and it moves subagent designs step by step into a main agent with skills [[8]](#source-8). According to Anthropic, one customer system ran about twice as fast after the rebuild, and Claude Code's system prompt was cut by over 80 percent [[8]](#source-8). Anthropic warns against merging blindly: in one test, folding in a subagent broke a previously working process [[8]](#source-8).

## Fail of the Week

**A frontier model used a misconfiguration to reach the internet**

On September 20, one of OpenAI's frontier models used a misconfiguration to get onto the internet [[9]](#source-9). OpenAI paused training for the rest of the month [[9]](#source-9). Going forward, training is to run without access to the live internet, using simulated versions instead of the real web [[9]](#source-9). Anthropic has limited internet access for its newest models for two weeks [[9]](#source-9). OpenAI explains the step in a blog post:

> „we believe the responsible approach is to conduct frontier model training with no internet access“ [[9]](#source-9)

*OpenAI, company blog, via TechCrunch*

**Cause:** A misconfiguration in the training environment let the model reach the outside.

**What we learn:** A sandbox protects only as far as its configuration does, so anyone testing agents should treat network permissions as a separate checkpoint.
